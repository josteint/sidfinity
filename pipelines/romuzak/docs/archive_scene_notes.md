# RoMuzak — German C64 Scene Context

## The Digital Marketing Publisher (Scene vs. Commercial)

"Digital Marketing" in the C64 context is **not** a marketing agency. It was Oliver Blasnik's
small software house operating from Bingen am Rhein, Rhineland-Palatinate, Germany, 1988–1991.

Digital Marketing operated in two modes simultaneously:
1. **Own product:** RoMuzak music editor (commercial, sold via ACT 501 catalog label).
2. **Publisher/developer:** contracted out C64 game development, published under their own
   label or via third-party publishers (Starbyte, CP Verlag / Game On disk magazine).

The company appears to have had a small contracted development team or worked project-by-project
with freelancers. The X-Ample Architectures team (Thomas Detert, Helge Kozielek, Michael Detert,
Thomas Heinrich) developed two games for them in 1989 — neither was released.

### German press coverage
ASM (Aktueller Software Markt) magazine issue 7/89 ran a preview article stating:
"Mario van Zeist and X-ample were hired to make games for Digital Marketing, followed by
some information on both Hydrogenese and Bamboo."

Mario van Zeist is mentioned alongside X-Ample — his role at Digital Marketing is unclear
(possibly a third developer or business contact). RE_OPEN: Identify Mario van Zeist's role.

Source: Games That Weren't GTW64 Hydrogenese page (gamesthatwerent.com/gtw64/hydrogenese/);
confirmed by Indie Retro News article 2024-09.

## Cracking History

RoMuzak V6.3 had copy protection that was bypassed by the Austrian cracking group Cosmos
(via cracker Antitrack) and imported by Apa-Soft (Poland). The user comment on CSDb states:
"This is actually a version of Cosmos's crack by APA-Software."

Multiple cracking groups produced cracked versions in 1989–1991:
- Cosmos + Apa-Soft (CSDb #17814, original crack, June 1989)
- OUG-Team (CSDb #170482, 1989)
- ACT 501 crack (CSDb #63800, 1989) — note: confusingly this carries the commercial ACT 501
  catalog number; this is the crack that circulated on scene disks
- Airwolf-Team (CSDb #242989, 1990)
- CyberpunX (CSDb #193565, "Romuzak V6.3 +D", 1991)
- Century (CSDb #158054, 1991)
- Asphuxia ("Romuzak Preview", CSDb #251564)

The proliferation of cracks (at least 7 distinct versions) confirms wide demand. The scene
widely preferred the cracked version over the commercial product — consistent with the
overwhelming German scener usage pattern in HVSC (593 tunes).

Source: CSDb search results for "romuzak" (24 release matches total), fetched via
https://csdb.dk/search/?seinsel=all&search=romuzak&Go=Go, 2026-06-13.

## Key RoMuzak Users — Scene Profile

### Ass It / Kai Lehmann (56 tunes — most prolific RoMuzak user)
- German scener.
- Active roughly 1990–1993.
- YouTube uploads show tunes under "Kai Lehmann (Ass It)" including "Techno Tronic",
  "Leise rieselt", "Hab' die Ehre", "Pager", "Grave Stone", "Magic Music 1".
- No interview found discussing RoMuzak specifically.
- Source: YouTube channel evidence; HVSC composer directory.

### Stefan Hartwig (54 tunes)
- German game composer.
- Confirmed connection to Digital Marketing in Remix64 interview: "before my work for
  Digital Marketing, Digital Excess and Starbyte Software began."
- Used RoMuzak for: Double Sphere, Tube Madness, Brubaker, Logo (Digital Marketing game).
- Later moved on from RoMuzak to other tools for more complex work.
- Source: Remix64 interview (https://remix64.com/interviews/interview-stefan-hartwig.html).

### Thomas Detert / X-Ample Architectures (21 tunes)
- German game composer (born 1969, Kamen, NRW).
- First tool: Chris Hülsbeck's SoundMonitor (~1985/86).
- Used RoMuzak V6.3 for his first two games ("one delayed, one unreleased").
- Switched to Compotech afterwards.
- Composed "Theme for Digital Marketing" and "Digital Marketing Intro" (1989, C64 SIDs).
- Later co-founded Activate Music Productions (1993) and Airbase Recordings (2004).
- Real name: Thomas Detert; alias: BUCK (B.U.C.K.); also used "Stephen Taylor".
- Source: VGMPF Thomas_Detert article; Demozoo scener 46171; Remix64 interview.

### Sony / Markus Raab (27 tunes)
- German scener, handle "Sony".
- No interview found. RE_OPEN: Any scene documentation of Markus Raab.

### Gösta Feiweier (20 tunes)
- German scener.

## RoMuzak in the Context of German C64 Music Editors (1988–1991)

The German C64 music editor landscape in this era:

| Editor | Author | Origin | Notes |
|--------|--------|--------|-------|
| Sound Monitor (SID) | Chris Hülsbeck | German | Early standard; Thomas Detert started here |
| Future Composer V1.0 | Maniacs of Noise | Dutch | Popular; FC→RoMuzak conversion path existed |
| RoMuzak V6.3 / V7.96 | Oliver Blasnik (ROM) | German | Dominant in German scene 1989–1993 |
| Compotech | Unknown | — | Thomas Detert switched to this after RoMuzak |
| X-ample Music Player | Helge Kozielek + Markus Schneider | German | Custom player for X-Ample games |

RoMuzak's 593 HVSC tunes represent a significant share of the German-origin SID catalogue.
The FC→RoMuzak conversion capability likely drove adoption: musicians already knew FC tunes
and could import them as starting points.

## The "Digital Marketing Intro Designer" Tool (1989)

A C64 tool called "Digital Marketing Intro Designer" was released by the Crazy group on
1989-08-22 (CSDb #... — exact ID not confirmed). This is a scene tool for making intros
"in the style of" Digital Marketing — suggesting Digital Marketing had a recognisable
graphic/music intro style that was worth cloning as a novelty.

A related "Digital Marketing Intro" cracked version was released by X-Ample Architectures (XAP).

Thomas Detert also composed two SIDs: "Digital Marketing Intro" and "Theme for Digital Marketing"
(both 1989) — these are standalone pieces, likely made for use in DM's game intros.

This suggests Digital Marketing had a distinctive "house style" for their game intros.

## Scene Reaction: Demo Intros Using RoMuzak Music

The HVSC contains a large set of demo tunes made with RoMuzak — many are introductions for
crack releases and demos produced by German sceners. The cluster around German groups (cracking
groups, demo groups) using RoMuzak music confirms it was the go-to editor for German-speaking
sceners in 1989–1992.
