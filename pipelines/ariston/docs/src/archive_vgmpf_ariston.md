# Ariston — Video Game Music Preservation Foundation Wiki

## Overview

"Ariston was a popular Commodore 64 music driver in the United Kingdom of the late 1980s."

## Development & Technical Details

- **Created:** 1987 by Ian Crabtree
- **Language:** 6502 Assembly
- **Key Contributors:** Wally Beben and Charles Deenen provided help and updates
- **Tuning Standards:** Compositions were tuned at 424 or 434 Hz (per various sources;
  the disassembled player has A4=459 Hz PAL / 477 Hz NTSC — see archive_jc64dis_disassembly.md)

## Evolution & Collaboration

In late 1987, Maniacs of Noise requested the source code from Beben to understand the
"phasing" effect. After receiving it, they enhanced the drums and returned the improved version.

## Editor & Composition

Philip Brabbin programmed the official Ariston Music Editor (1988). Almost all composers
arranged in a 6502 assembler rather than the GUI editor, "leading to multiple modified
driver versions."

## Platform Expansion

Wally Beben ported Ariston to Atari ST and Amiga in 1988 with assistance from Chris,
a game programmer from Bury St Edmunds, Suffolk.

## Composers

Eight notable composers used Ariston:
- Allister Brimble
- Barry Leitch
- Ian Crabtree
- Jonathan Dunn
- Matt Gray
- Steve Barrett
- Wally Beben
- Paul Meredith

## Games Known to Use Ariston

- RoboCop (C64) — Jonathan Dunn (one of his "first two games")
- Marauder (C64) — Barry Leitch
- Subterranea (C64) — Jonathan Dunn (delayed)
- Match Day II (C64) — Jonathan Dunn (possibly)
- Total Eclipse (C64) — Ian Crabtree (1989 Quantum Designs) — note: Wally Beben's version
- Plus demos/music releases from Archangel Developments (Ian Crabtree's own label)
