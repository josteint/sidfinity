# Ariston Driver — Composers and Known Games

## Confirmed Ariston Users

### Ian Crabtree (driver author)
- Works through his own label "Archangel Developments" (1987–1989)
- Also worked with "Quantum Designs" (Total_Eclipse.sid, 1989)
- HVSC SID directory: `MUSICIANS/C/Crabtree_Ian/`
- Known SIDs: Angel_Meadows (1987), Damnation_Driveway (1987), Going_Home (1987),
  Going_Home_II (1987), Going_Home_III (1988), Kraxxon_Zone (1987), Midnight_Shiver (1987),
  Moun (1988), Outrun (1988), Plastic_Butter (1988), Technicolour_1 (?), Total_Eclipse (1989),
  Dudges_Demo (1987-88), Frantic (collab with Roy Fielding/Toy)
- Tuning: 433.5 Hz PAL, 450 Hz NTSC (per VGMPF; "sound the same on every SID chip")
- Used The Ariston Music Editor except on Summer Olympiad where he likely used assembly
  via Wally Beben's variant

### Wally Beben
- Original collaborator; helped develop the driver
- Ported Ariston to Atari ST and Amiga in 1988 (with game programmer "Chris" from Bury St Edmunds)
- Created "Ariston+Samples" demo/release (1987)
- His variant "Wally_Beben" includes improved drums (from Maniacs of Noise exchange)
- Amiga composition workflow: Soundtracker → converted to blocks → his own player
  (migrated/converted from C64 player for processor/memory efficiency)
- Composition: "Dark Side" (1988, Incentive) uses the Wally_Beben Ariston variant
- Filter: Used SID unstable filter on only one game: Total Eclipse
- Location: Thetford, Norfolk (1991 BBS: "Sounds Digital")

### Jonathan Dunn
- Used Ariston for his "first two games" (of which Subterranea was delayed)
- RoboCop (C64) uses Ariston; the theme was also used in a British TV Ariston washing
  machine advert ~1992/3 ("Ariston…and on…and on and on and Ariston")
- Access route unknown ("It is unknown how Ariston was available to Dunn and other users")
- Later moved to Martin Galway's driver, then Paul Hughes' driver

### Barry Leitch
- Used Ariston on Marauder (C64, The Sales Curve 1988)
- Access route unknown ("It is unknown how he got it")
- Later used a driver built by a programmer friend (Turbo Ass based)

### Matt Gray
- "Initially, Gray used Soundmonitor and the Ariston driver, sometimes optimized by game
  programmers and at that sometimes with declined quality."
- Codemasters convinced him to write his own driver
- No specific games identified as using Ariston

### Steve Barrett
- "Barrett used Ariston" (single VGMPF entry, no game list)
- Known for Super Hang-On and ~28 other C64 titles

### Allister Brimble
- Used "Wally Beben's driver" early in career (per Remix64 interview)
- The interview uses "Wally Beben's driver" not explicitly "Ariston" but this is the
  Beben variant of Ariston
- Later used a custom editor by Michael Delaney

### Paul Meredith
- Listed as Ariston user by VGMPF (no games listed in available sources)

### Mark Wilson
- Listed as having used The Ariston Music Editor (per VGMPF The_Ariston_Music_Editor page)
- Also: Kev Bruce and Ian Crabtree listed as direct users of the editor GUI

## Notes on Driver Distribution

The driver was UK commercial / semi-private. It circulated among UK game developers
in the late 1980s through informal channels (not a public release). The cracked version
was distributed on the demo scene in mid-1988 (CSDb #29914, #119920).

Ian Crabtree resided in Newbold, Greater Manchester (1988). Philip Brabbin's location
unknown. Wally Beben in Thetford, Norfolk.

---

## Atari ST / Amiga Port

Wally Beben ported Ariston to Atari ST and Amiga in 1988 with help from "Chris" (a game
programmer friend from Bury St Edmunds, Suffolk). The Atari Forum thread (atari-forum.com/
viewtopic.php?t=21588) documents a reverse-engineering effort of Beben's ST R-Type music
replayer, circa 2004 — by "Mug UK". The note data format is not documented in the thread,
but Beben's Amiga workflow used Soundtracker as input then converted to his own block format.
The ST format has not been found in ModLand/UADE archives or ExoticA in this search.
