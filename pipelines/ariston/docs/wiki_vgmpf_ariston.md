# Ariston — Video Game Music Preservation Foundation Wiki

## Summary

Ariston was a Commodore 64 music driver created in **1987** by **Ian Crabtree** (with help and updates from **Wally Beben**). It became popular in the UK during the late 1980s.

## Development history

- Ian Crabtree created the driver in 1987; Wally Beben contributed help and updates.
- In **late 1987**, Maniacs of Noise asked Beben how he did the "phasing" effect.
  After Beben sent them the source code, they added **better drums** and sent it back.
- In **1988**, Beben ported Ariston to **Atari ST and Amiga** with help from "a very good
  friend, game programmer Chris from Bury St Edmunds, Suffolk."

## Technical details

- Written in **6502 Assembly language**.
- Multiple modified versions exist, **all tuned at 424 or 434 Hz**.
- Ian Crabtree's own C64 soundtracks are tuned at **433.5 Hz on PAL, 450 Hz on NTSC**,
  and "sound the same on every SID chip" (no chip-to-chip filter variance).
- Most composers arranged music **directly in a 6502 assembler** — they did NOT use the
  GUI editor as the primary workflow.
- **Philip Brabbin** programmed the official Ariston Music Editor GUI (cracked and
  distributed 24 June 1988 by Criminals in Computers + Illusion, CSDb #29914 / #119920).
- An additional contributor listed in the VGMPF source: **Charles Deenen** (although
  Deenen is better known for his own C64 driver; this may refer to late use by Barry
  Leitch of both Ariston and Deenen's driver at different points in his career — needs
  verification).

## Associated composers (eight total per VGMPF)

| Composer | Notes |
|---|---|
| Ian Crabtree | Created the driver; used the Ariston Music Editor except on Summer Olympiad (assembly-direct via Beben's variant) |
| Wally Beben | Co-author; used Ariston on C64 and ported it to Atari ST + Amiga |
| Allister Brimble | First used Beben's driver (= Ariston); later switched to Michael Delaney's custom driver |
| Barry Leitch | Used Ariston for **Marauder** (C64); later used a custom driver (Turbo Ass), then Axel Brown's driver |
| Jonathan Dunn | Used Ariston on his **first two games** (incl. Subterranea); then Martin Galway's driver; then Paul Hughes' driver |
| Matt Gray | Initially used **Soundmonitor and the Ariston driver**, sometimes optimised by game programmers; then wrote own driver (Codemasters persuaded him to) |
| Steve Barrett | Used Ariston (no specific games identified in sources) |
| Paul Meredith | Listed; no details available |
| Mark Wilson | Listed; no details available |

(VGMPF lists eight total; the table above enumerates nine names across sources — one may be a duplication or source error; "Charles Deenen" mention needs cross-check.)

## Platform expansion

- C64 original (1987)
- Atari ST port (1988) — Beben + "Chris from Bury St Edmunds, Suffolk"
- Amiga port (1988) — same collaboration

## Known games

- **Summer Olympiad** (C64, Tynesoft 1988) — Ian Crabtree; likely assembly-direct using Beben's Ariston variant
- **Marauder** (C64) — Barry Leitch
- **Subterranea** (C64) — Jonathan Dunn (first two games used Ariston)
- Matt Gray's early Codemasters titles (Driller 1987, Hunter's Moon 1987, Quedex 1987, etc.) — Ariston used early, before own driver

## How availability worked

"It is unknown how Ariston was available to Dunn and other users." — the driver appears
to have circulated informally between UK game composers via personal contacts (Compunet,
direct sharing).
