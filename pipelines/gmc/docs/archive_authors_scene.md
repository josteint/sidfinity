# GMC / Game Music Creator — Author, Group, and Hungarian Scene

## 1. Balázs Farkas (Brian) — Biographical Summary

**Handle:** Brian  
**Real name:** Balázs Farkas  
**Nationality:** Hungarian (Budapest, Hungary)  
**HVSC Musicians.txt entry:** `Brian (Farkas, Balázs) / Graffity - HUNGARY`  
**CSDb scener ID:** 367  
**Demozoo scener ID:** 1711  
**Alternative handles:** The Softbuster Kid 1988 (SBK), BPStudio (later years)  

### Timeline of group memberships
| Period | Group |
|--------|-------|
| 1988 (solo) | The Softbuster Kid (SBK) — his first one-man group |
| 1988–1990 | Tomcat (left August 1990) |
| Sep 1990–Oct 1994 | **Graffity** (co-founder) |
| 1993–present | The Imperium Arts (TIA) |
| Earlier affiliations (exact dates uncertain) | Absolute!, Axioma, Soc. Brigade, Toxic Volume, Reflex |

### Roles
Brian worked as both **coder** and **musician** throughout his C64 career. He is the sole
author of the GMC music player engine and the primary coder of the DMC series. His
compositional style is represented in 33 SID files in HVSC under the GMC format (the
engine's creator ranks 4th by tune count among all GMC composers).

### Career arc (reconstructed from Demozoo + CSDb + Tehernapló interview)
- **1988–1989:** Solo work as "The Softbuster Kid"; first demoscene experiments.
- **1989:** Appears in "Demo of the Year 1990" (actually Nov 1989 release) as graphician
  under Actual Trading Generation.
- **1990 (early):** In Tomcat, Brian created **Eqaleditor V1** (a music tool for Caution
  group, a proto-tool preceding GMC). Also created the Tomcat Notemaker, which according
  to the Tehernapló interview influenced later editors such as "Facenoter."
- **1990 (mid-late):** Left Tomcat in August 1990. Co-founded Graffity with Jay, Matrix,
  and Maxwell in October 1990.
- **1990 (Dec):** Released GMC V1.0 ("Superiors Game Music Creator System V1") at CSDb #7268
  — co-coded with Jay; graphics by Jay; 27 bundled demo SIDs.
- **1990 (Dec):** Released GMC V1.6 (editor-only + beta music, CSDb #98639).
- **1991:** Rapid pivot from GMC to DMC; DMC V1.2 (Feb 1991) → V2.0 (Feb 1991) → V4.0
  (Sep 1991). The Superiors branding persisted through DMC V2.1+.
- **1991–1993:** Active C64 demo coder + musician for Graffity; music for external groups
  (Cherubs, Excess, Delta System, etc.).
- **1993:** Moved primary activity to The Imperium Arts; left active C64 demo making.
- **1993 onwards (PC):** AMC 3.0 (AdLib Music Creator, PC); demos for Axioma, Hype, etc.
- **1996:** "Breath of Air" (C64); "Higher Love" (C64 demo by Graffity).
- **2018:** DMC V6.0 released (under The Imperium Arts) — modern revival of the DMC engine.
- **2023:** Music for "JustinBlue" (Graffity's long-awaited mega-demo, finally released Feb 2023).
- **2025:** Still active (attended T0AD 2025 party).

### Tehernapló interview (2013-05-28/29) — key quotes translated from Hungarian

The Tehernapló blog conducted a two-part retrospective interview with Graffity members
in May 2013. The interview covers GMC directly:

> "We had such a hair-brained idea that we could potentially sell this thing [GMC] to
> Magic-Disk magazine. But the publication declined our offer."  
> — Brian (on the commercial aspiration behind GMC)

The interview notes that GMC and the early DMC were created within Graffity's internal
"Superiors Aural Department (SAD)" subdivision — explaining the "Superiors" branding in
the tool's subtitle and the SIDid pattern name "GMC/Superiors."

Brian is described as Graffity's "primary programmer and musician" who:
- Created both GMC (1990) and DMC (1991 onwards)
- Created the Tomcat Notemaker (pre-Graffity), which influenced later editors
- "Wrote music for the group and later contributed only music as other coders surpassed
  his programming abilities" (by the mid-1990s)
- Had "exceptional technical insight and distinctive personality"
- Left active C64 demoscene participation around 1993

Brian's quote on DMC's accidental success:
> "It seemed like a planned brand, but it wasn't." The tool emerged from necessity rather
> than deliberate marketing strategy.

### Brian's GitHub presence
GitHub handle `megazyz` appears to belong to a different Balázs Farkas (Minecraft/.NET).
The GMC/DMC Brian has never released source code publicly. Source NOT on GitHub.

**Sósperec note:** The interview mentions a competing music editor by Trays (Graffity)
called "Sósperec" (Hungarian: pretzel-like snack), described as "a minimalist music editor
occupying only 8 raster rows of memory" that was kept proprietary and distributed only to
selected musicians. This tool is NOT GMC/DMC — it is an independent, unreleased tool that
never entered public circulation. It does not appear in HVSC.

### "Superiors" branding — what it means
The term "Superiors" in "GMC/Superiors" (SIDid) and "Superiors Game Music Creator System
V1" (full title) refers to **Graffity's internal branding identity**, not a separate cracker
or spread group. The Tehernapló interview describes it as the "Superiors Aural Department
(SAD)" — an internal subdivision of Graffity focused on music tool development. This
"Superiors" label carried over into the early DMC: the first transitional release was
named "The Superiors Demo Music Creator V2.1+" (Demozoo, 1991). By DMC V1.2 the
"Superiors" subtitle was dropped and the tool was known simply as "Demo Music Creator."

The Recollection cracker map for Hungary lists no group called "Superiors" — confirming
it was never a standalone cracking group, only a Graffity internal label.

## 3. Hungarian C64 Demoscene Context

### Key groups in the Hungarian scene (circa 1988–1994)
From the Recollection cracker map and scene.hu historical articles:

- **Quality** — one of Hungary's first C64 groups; Jay came from here to Graffity
- **FBI Crew** — early group
- **Chromance** (CSDb #250) — active Hungarian group
- **Leader** (CSDb #778) — Hungarian group
- **Resource** (CSDb #113) — Hungarian group
- **Faces** — Brian composed music for their 1991 demo "City Heat"
- **Singular** (CSDb #514, founded Sep 1995) — successor-generation group; still active
  2026; created FunkPaint, VI65, 64TASS tools. NOT connected to GMC/Graffity directly.
- **Singular Crew** (demoscene.hu) — same as above; founded by Poison, Cargo, Grachow

### Economic context — Hungarian C64 access
From the scene.hu historical article on the Hungarian demoscene (2008):
> *"The C64 was procured through 'shopping tourism' to Austria, often smuggled back in car
> trunks. A C64 with disk drive was worth more than some vehicles at the time."*

This socialist-era scarcity explains why the Hungarian scene developed distinctly from
Western European scenes — tools like GMC were created locally rather than imported.

### Brian's place in the scene
- Graffity was described by scene.hu as *"the most iconic Hungarian C64 team of the
  first half of the 1990s."*
- DMC became described as *"the most successful C64 music editor ever created"* — emerging
  directly from GMC's codebase.
- Brian attended demoscene parties including T0AD 2025, Function 2020, Scenest 1995/1996,
  Headway and Faces Party 1993, Art-X Party — confirming lifelong scene involvement.

### Singular Crew and GMC
Singular Crew (founded 1995) is the leading CURRENT Hungarian C64 group but has no
documented connection to Graffity or GMC/DMC. Their primary tools (FunkPaint, 64TASS)
are independent of the Graffity tool lineage. GMC/DMC users in HVSC include Hungarian
sceners but the composer/coder Wacek (top GMC tune count at 97) is Polish (from Arise/WST).
