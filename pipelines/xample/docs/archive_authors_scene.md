## Group: X-Ample Architectures (XAP)

**Founded:** July 1988, Germany  
**Closed:** 1997 (reconstituted briefly as Büttner GmbH 1997–2000)  
**CSDb:** https://csdb.dk/group/?id=245  
**Demozoo:** https://demozoo.org/groups/2825/  
**Homepage (historical):** http://www.xap64.de/ (now offline; Wayback access not available)  
**Aliases:** Xample, X-Ample, X, [X]

The group name stands for "Example," as stated in their 1988 release *Blade Runner*. Their motto: "Bit For Bit A Hit."

### Founding Context

X-Ample began as two parallel demo groups — **Omega 8** (Thomas Detert, Michael Satzer/Detert, Helge Kozielek) and **X-Ample Architectures** — founded in summer 1988. They rapidly merged/converged, with X-Ample evolving from a demo group into one of the most active C64 game development teams of the early 1990s.

Their distribution strategy was via **CP-Verlag** magazine covermount disks (*Magic Disk 64*, *Game On*, *Golden Disk 64*), which gave them wide reach but limited scene visibility (mainstream magazines like *64'er* viewed them as competitors).

### Members

**Core founders (July 1988):**
- Stephen Taylor (musician) — handle used by Thomas Detert early on
- Takashi (coder/musician/graphician)
- General X (graphician)
- Chap Bizarre (coder/graphician) — also credited as co-author of Compotech V2.1

**Key members joined later:**
- **Markus Schneider** (coder, musician) — joined March 1989 from Lords of Sonics
- **Helge Kozielek** (games/sound programmer)
- **Joachim Fräder** (coder) — programmed Compotech editor surface
- **Joachim Multermann** (coder)
- **Thomas Heinrich** (graphician)
- **Michael Detert / Michael Satzer** (graphician — brother of Thomas Detert)
- **Mario van Zeist** — optimised player CPU speed (Dutch, external collaborator)
- **Michael Büttner** — later management

**Former members:**
- Mr. Cursor, The Viking, Tomcat

**Thomas Detert** (musician) was a co-founder; his brother Michael Detert was the main graphician. Thomas used the handles B.U.C.K. (until June 1988) and Stephen Taylor (1988–1989) before using his real name.

## Markus Schneider

**Born:** 3 November 1970, Winsen, Lower Saxony, West Germany  
**Handles:** Diflex, DFX, Synth-Man, MS, M.S, Sym  
**CSDb:** https://csdb.dk/scener/?id=6003  
**VGMPF:** https://www.vgmpf.com/Wiki/index.php?title=Markus_Schneider

**Role in X-Ample:** Coder + musician. Primary author of the sound driver / player engine.

### Career Timeline

- **1984–1987:** Received C64; entered demo scene
- **1987–1988:** Started composing with SoundMonitor; met Jens Blidon at school
- **1988:** Co-founded **Lords of Sonics** with Jens Blidon. Wrote Blidon a better sound driver — this took ~2 months and became the seed of all future X-Ample player technology
- **1988–1989:** Wrote The Parsec Music Editor (V5.1, released by Mnemonic Designs 1989), a collaborative effort with Nic, ADT, and SMC
- **1989 (March):** Joined X-Ample Architectures as coder + musician
- **1989:** Spent ~7 weeks integrating his driver with X-Ample technology → the Compotech lineage begins
- **1990:** Used Chris Hülsbeck's TFMX (special version provided by Hülsbeck) for Amiga music (Tusker)
- **1992:** Compotech editor released
- **1995:** Compotech V2.1 released; *Thomas Detert & Markus Schneider Music Collection (160 Tracks)* released
- **1998:** Co-founded Skycom GmbH (IT company)
- **2002:** Began remixing C64 music
- **2005:** Co-founded Symphonic Dreams division with Rob Hubbard

### Game Music Credits (C64, selection)

- Babylon Four (1988, with Jens Blidon — Lords of Sonics era)
- No Mercy (1989)
- Timezone (1989, sound effects + music driver — used "ROM's Fix" for SFX)
- Dick Tracy (1990, arranged)
- Gordian Tomb (1990, with Thomas Detert)
- The Yawn (1990, with Stefan Hartwig)
- ZoZoom, Stümp, Tit Bit, Crystal Fever (1990)
- Turn It II, Th!nk Cross, Stone Edge, Apoxoly, Gilded Age (1991)
- Rolling Ronny, Lethal Zone (1991)
- Xiphoids, Magic Mouse in Goblin Land (1992)

### Groups

- Lords of Sonics (co-founder, 1988 →)
- Elite (member)
- X-Ample Architectures (member, March 1989 →)
- Also credited in releases for: Crazy, Triad, Crest, Sodom, Double Density, Kingsoft, Ascon Software, Byteriders, Bones Park Software Artistic

## Thomas Detert

**Born:** 26 November 1969, Kamen, North Rhine-Westphalia, West Germany  
**Handles:** B.U.C.K (until June 1988), Stephen Taylor (1988–1989)  
**CSDb:** https://csdb.dk/scener/?id=1312  
**Demozoo:** https://demozoo.org/sceners/46171/  
**VGMPF:** https://www.vgmpf.com/Wiki/index.php/Thomas_Detert  
**Website:** xap64.de (group history page)

**Role in X-Ample:** Co-founder, primary composer. Used the X-Ample player (Compotech) for all game music.

### Career Timeline

- **1986/87:** Began C64 composition with Chris Hülsbeck's SoundMonitor
- **1988 (summer):** Co-founded Omega 8 and X-Ample Architectures with Michael Satzer/Detert and Helge Kozielek
- **First two commercial games:** Used **RoMuzak V6.3** (not yet the X-Ample player)
- **From ~1989 onward:** Used Compotech as his primary C64 composition tool
- Also used **X-Tracker V4.00 Beta** (1996, credit on that tool)
- Also used **MFL-Editor V4.0** (1992, Extreme group's tool)
- **1993:** Founded Activate Music Productions
- **1994:** Formed eurodance act Activate; later 666
- **2004:** Co-founded Airbase Recordings

### Key musical influences

Martin Galway, Rob Hubbard, Jeroen Tel; and synthesizer composers: Vangelis, Jean-Michel Jarre, Howard Jones, Art of Noise, Pet Shop Boys. Inspired specifically by Galway's arrangement of Jarre's *Magnetic Fields, Part 4* on C64.

### Game Music Credits (C64, selection; 35+ games)

- Blue Angel 69 (1989, Blue Byte — first commercial game)
- Gordian Tomb (1990) — over 30 minutes of music, weeks to complete; regarded as his best
- B-Bobs, Another World, Coalminer, Eskimo Games (1990)
- Starforce, Clystron, P.P. Hammer and his Pneumatic Weapon (1991)
- Stone Age, Tales of Boon (1992–1993)
- Mega Starforce, Parsec (game), Quadrant (1993)

Thomas Detert did NOT compose Turrican or Katakis — those are Chris Hülsbeck's. Detert's output suffered recognition issues because his games were CP-Verlag covermount titles, not high-profile retail releases.

## Helge Kozielek

**Role:** Games and sound programmer; optimised the X-Ample player CPU performance  
**CSDb/Demozoo:** Former member of X-Ample Architectures  
**Country:** Germany

Helge Kozielek was one of the three co-founders of X-Ample (along with Thomas Detert and Michael Satzer). He and Markus Schneider together wrote the original X-Ample Sound Player (Version 3.2 — see player_routine.txt). Mario van Zeist made further speed optimisations. Kozielek also coded several X-Ample games (notably the unreleased Hydrogenese).

---

## Joachim Fräder

**Role:** Editor UI programmer  
**CSDb:** Co-author of Compotech V2.1 (1995)  
**Country:** Germany

Joachim Fräder programmed the editor surface (the user interface / front-end) of Compotech. The player/driver code was Markus Schneider's and Helge Kozielek's; Fräder handled the editor shell. Also credited alongside Chap Bizarre and Markus Schneider in the Compotech V2.1 CSDb entry. He coded X-Ample games: Starforce (1991), Snowman (CP Verlag, 1992).

## Jens Blidon

**Role:** Musician, Lords of Sonics co-founder  
**CSDb:** https://csdb.dk/scener/?id=2205

Met Markus Schneider at school. Jens was composing on Soundmonitor; Schneider wrote him a better driver (~2 months, 1988) — this is the origin of the X-Ample player lineage. Blidon has been active in the C64 scene continuously from 1989 to 2025 (400+ credited works) but never joined X-Ample Architectures directly.

## Tufan Uysal (SoNiC)

**CSDb / VGMPF entry:** XTracker V4 author; NOT a member of X-Ample Architectures

Tufan Uysal is the author of **The Ultimate X-Tracker V4.13** (1996, The Art Project Studios). He used Compotech from 1996 and created his own Compotech variants. The "X" in XTracker likely refers to "X-Ample" stylistically but XTracker is a separate product by a separate author. The SIDId fingerprint for XTracker_V4.1x and V4.2x shares the family signature with the core X-Ample player but diverges in the inner loop structure. Tufan Uysal also worked on Turrican III (C64) music arrangement and later composed for commercial PC/console games.

## Stefan Hartwig

**Role:** C64 composer; HVSC entry: `Hartwig_Stefan/`; ~134 SIDs

Stefan Hartwig composed primarily Turrican-related music. His HVSC SIDs are classified as Compotech_V2.x / Thomas_Detert / X-Ample variants in SIDId. He is NOT documented as a member of X-Ample Architectures but shared their player. Hartwig's interview (Remix64) does not mention Compotech directly; his technical workflow centred on Casio CZ1, Korg M1, Roland U110 hardware with Logic Audio Platinum software later on.

## Scene Context: German C64 Scene

X-Ample operated in the German C64 scene 1988–1997. Key German scene events they attended: **RADWAR Party** (Artists of Time placed 3rd at Radwar Party 2, 1988). They explicitly saw themselves as a professional game development outfit rather than a pure underground demo group. Quote from Thomas Detert: "We (X-Ample Architectures) have never seen ourselves as an underground group, although we did a lot of demos on the C64."

The group's final reunion demo *Veterans of Style* (2017) demonstrates sustained identity across decades.
