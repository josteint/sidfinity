## DRAX (Jesper Olsen) CSDb comment — confirmed wider scene adoption

From CSDb #90307 (Soundmaster V3.1) comments:

DRAX (5 Sep 2010):
> "I also did a couple of tunes in this editor... and I still trying to find them... Might have sent them to some nato or noise members back then... Maduplec? ;)"

DRAX is a prolific and highly-regarded Danish C64 musician (Jesper Olsen, Crest / Vibrants). His mention of "nato or noise members" refers to the Danish demo groups NATO and Noise. This confirms **Soundmaster V3.1 spread into the wider Scandinavian/European demo scene** beyond just Fire-Eagle and Soedesoft by 1989–1990. The handle "Maduplec" is a known NATO member.

Stainless Steel (5 Jun 2010):
> "I actually liked this one back then."

Stainless Steel (Albin Hjärtström, Swedish scene) also used it. Brief but confirms cross-border use.

## "Magic Disk 64" distribution

CSDb confirms Soundmaster V3.1 was distributed via **Magic Disk 64** — a well-known German C64 floppy magazine (bi-monthly, ~1987–1993). Distribution through Magic Disk 64 guaranteed wide European reach, explaining why 929+ HVSC tunes use the engine despite it never being a commercial product. German users' encounter with the tool via Magic Disk 64 also explains the existence of the German PDF manual ("Walter's" documentation).

## Reconstruction: scene distribution chronology

1. **1988:** Jeroen Soede writes the player routine; Michiel Soede writes the editor. SoedeSound Editor V1.0 circulates within Fire-Eagle.
2. **Feb 1989:** Soundmaster V1.0 released publicly by Fire-Eagle on disk. Rage for Order imports it to North America (Toronto) by 5 March 1989.
3. **1989:** Soundmaster V3.1 published in Magic Disk 64, reaching all of German-speaking Europe and beyond. Walter writes a German manual. DRAX and other scene musicians adopt it.
4. **~1989–1990:** "Soede Editor" (successor, larger GUI, variable bar length) circulates. Hacked variants emerge ("TURBO GTI SSS" etc.).
5. **1992:** SoedeSound Editor V1.1 — minor revision, circulated in scene.
6. **1994:** SoundMaster II (Amiga) used for *C64 Memories Vol. IV* musicdisk.

The 929 HVSC tunes span this entire period. The V3.1 / Magic Disk 64 distribution is the most likely source of the majority of HVSC tunes since it had the widest reach.
