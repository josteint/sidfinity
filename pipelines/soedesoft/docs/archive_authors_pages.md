## Authors: Jeroen Soede and Michiel Soede

### Identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Jeroen Soede** | C64 player/routine programmer |
| **Michiel Soede** | C64 editor programmer + music composer |
| Relationship | Twin brothers |
| Born | 21 November 1970 (both) |
| Country | Netherlands |
| Scene handle | "SoedeSoft" (used as group name, not individual handle) |
| Jeroen as of 2001 | Plays electric lead guitar |

**Division of labour** (from Michiel Soede interview, Remix64, 13 April 2001):
> "My brother created the routine, and I created an editor (everything from scratch,
> nothing was ripped)."

Jeroen focused on melodies and composed most of SoedeSoft's HVSC music (38 SIDs
attributed to him). Michiel preferred "experimenting with strange sounds."

## Group Affiliations

### Fire-Eagle (1987–1989)

Both Soedes were members of **Fire-Eagle**, a Dutch-based Commodore 64 group
that disbanded by September 1989 (Atomic News #5: "FIRE EAGLE IS DEAD").

- **Parent group:** The Highest Level
- **Country:** Netherlands (with Danish and German members)
- **Total productions on Demozoo:** 39 (1986–1989)
- **Notable Fire-Eagle members alongside Soedes:** Access (DK), Nigel (DK),
  Rog (NL), Roger Rabbit (DE), The Mummy (NL), Xerox (DK)
- **Collaborative affiliates:** Genesis Project (19+ joint productions),
  Sphinx, Science 451
- **Demozoo entry:** https://demozoo.org/groups/7594/
- **Status:** Soedesoft is listed as a subgroup of Fire-Eagle on both CSDb and Demozoo

The V1.0 release of Soundmaster (February 1989) was distributed under the
Fire-Eagle + Rage for Order + EGO joint label, making Fire-Eagle the original
distribution vehicle for the tracker.

### Soedesoft (1985–1989+)

- **Type:** Demo group + music group (founders = only full members)
- **Country:** Netherlands
- **Official website:** http://www.soedesoft.com (active as of 2026 — modern
  commercial SIDmaster plugin for Reason)
- **CSDb entry:** https://csdb.dk/group/?id=1815 (user rating 8.2/10 from 9 votes)
- **Demozoo entry:** https://demozoo.org/groups/7598/
- **Pouet entry:** https://www.pouet.net/groups.php?which=8188 (added 23 Dec 2007)
- **Total CSDb releases as Soedesoft:** 19 (1987–1989)
- **CSDb notes:** "For some time they were a sub-group of Fire-Eagle"

### Remix64 Interview (13 April 2001, by Neil Carr)
Source: https://remix64.com/interviews/interview-michiel-soede-www-soedesoft-com.html
Key excerpts:

**On influences:**
> "Rob Hubbard was my favorite... copying his famous drum sounds was one of the first
> things I did. I also liked Ben Daglish, and David Whittaker."

**On the C64's limitations and strengths:**
> "Limited to using 3 voices, and the limited amount of waveforms... only had one
> controllable filter." Appreciated "ring-modulation, synchronisation."

**On Soundmaster's origin:**
> After finding Chris Huelsbeck's Soundmonitor inadequate ("the creation of sounds was
> too limited and the size of the music was too much"), Michiel and Jeroen built from
> scratch. "My brother created the routine, and I created an editor (everything from
> scratch, nothing was ripped)."

**On the Amiga successor:**
> They developed "SoundMaster II, which was based on our C64 routine" and later used
> "MIDI on the Amiga (Bars 'n Pipes)."

**On notable Amiga-era works:**
- Nemesis the Warlock (8-year project, note-for-note Kawai K4 cover)
- StarPaws cover
- Fifth Axis (house version)
- "Vinnie" (personal favourite, named after Yamaha AN1x synth sound)

**Scene contacts mentioned:** Peter Sanden (HVSC), Wilfred Bos, Neil Carr

## Modern Continuation: SoedeSoft.com and SIDmaster

The soedesoft.com domain remained active (confirmed 2026). Michiel Soede continues
to develop under the SoedeSoft name. The **SIDmaster** Rack Extension plugin for
Reason (first released ca. 2011, current v1.4) explicitly implements:

> "effects used in the old days of the C64 based on SoedeSoft's original music
> routine of the 80's (such as arpeggios, wave patterns, modulating the pulse width
> or filter)"

Version history of SIDmaster plugin:
| Version | Changes |
|---|---|
| 1.06 | Initial release |
| 1.08 | Bugfix |
| 1.1 | New UI, frequency modulation |
| 1.2 | 8580 support, Combinator patches |
| 1.3 | Sound quality improvements, filter enhancements |
| 1.4 | GUI updates, pulse envelope feature |

Available on Reason Studios shop:
https://www.reasonstudios.com/shop/rack-extension/soedesoft-sidmaster/

  BBS-era documentation were recovered from Wayback in this session.

## Leads to Follow

1. **Wayback Machine — soedesoft.com snapshots.** The fetcher was blocked from
   web.archive.org. A direct browser visit to
   `https://web.archive.org/web/*/soedesoft.com` would reveal any archived
   Soundmaster documentation pages, source listings, or old product pages that
   Michiel Soede may have published on his personal site in the 2000s.

2. **Soundmaster V3.2 — CSDb id=117086.** The CSDb page for V3.2 was confirmed
   to exist but was unreachable (503 during fetch). It is described as "only for
   members from Fire-Eagle" / "not officially released." The binary file likely
   differs from V3.1 in the portamento path (per sidid byte signature analysis in
   sidid_signature_analysis.md). Direct fetch when CSDb is available.

3. **S.F.X. Editor (1989).** This is a confirmed separate SoedeSoft tool
   (Demozoo lists it). Its CSDb release ID was not recovered. It presumably
   produced SID sound effects compatible with the Soundmaster player. Worth
   finding: does it share the same player binary? Does it produce separate SFX
   data blocks?

4. **SoedeSound Editor V1.1 (1992).** CSDb scener page for Michiel Soede
   mentions "SoedeSound Editor V1.1" (1992) in the tools listing. This is a
   post-scene release not covered by any docs here. It may represent a late-1980s
   to early-1990s continuation of the editor that is separate from the V3.x
   Soundmaster releases. CSDb search for this exact title.

5. **Soundmaster V2.x gap.** No V2.0 or V2.1 releases appear on CSDb or anywhere
   else. The jump from V1.0 (Feb 1989) to V3.1 (1989) leaves the V2 generation
   unaccounted. Either versions 2.x were never publicly released (internal), were
   relabelled into the V3.x sequence, or were released under an alias. The V3.2
   being labelled as "1988" in CSDb suggests the version numbers may not be
   chronological in the normal sense.

6. **Soede Editor V4.0 / Turbo GTI SSS.** The SID Preservation tracker page
   mentions "Soede Editor V4.0" (expanded GUI, variable bar lengths) and
   "Soede Editor Turbo GTI SSS" (custom group modifications). These do not appear
   on CSDb under those names. They may be very late (post-1990) or Amiga-era
   successors, or they may be scene-internal names for custom-patched versions.
   No HVSC SIDs appear to use a V4.0 player signature (no sidid sub-tag for it).

7. **Michiel Soede direct contact.** He was reachable via soedesoft.com as of
   the 2001 interview and remains commercially active. A direct enquiry about the
   V2.x gap, S.F.X. Editor format, and internal V3.2 differences could resolve
   several OPENs in one email.

8. **DRAX's Soundmaster tunes.** DRAX (Thomas Mogensen) mentions composing "a
   couple of tunes" in Soundmaster and trying to find them. These SIDs may exist
   in CSDb under DRAX's scener page but not tagged with the SoedeSoft engine in
   HVSC (if they were stored as raw-player saves rather than relocated SIDs).
   Worth searching CSDb scener id for DRAX + SoedeSoft cross-reference.
