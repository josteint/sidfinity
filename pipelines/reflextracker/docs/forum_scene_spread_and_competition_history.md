# Reflextracker — Scene Spread and Competition History

## Origin (1995)

- Released by **Reflex + The Obsessed Maniacs**, both German C64 groups
- Principal author: **kb (Tammo Hinrichs)** — code, with Quiss (Matthias Kramm) and Zorc
- PVCF (Kai Walter) designed the tool's workflow, wrote documentation (BESCHREIBUNG), and
  composed the three bundled demo songs (Access Denied remix, Gubber, Trance 202)
- Released ca. 1995 (user memory; the demo songs are dated 1994-1995)

## Distribution via Brainbeat Musicdisks

Primary distribution route: bundled with Brainbeat musicdisk releases:
- **Brainbeat** (Aug 1993, CSDb #6829) — predates Reflextracker; PVCF's early compositions
- **Brainbeat II** (1994, CSDb #4713) — transition period
- **Brainbeat III** (1995, CSDb #6830) — confirmed: "I got it right away in 1995 or 1996,
  together with ReflexTracker on another disk." Reflextracker distributed alongside this release.
- **Brainbeat IV** (1995, CSDb #60437) — 13 songs by PVCF and Beathoven; 
  PVCF confirms "Brainbeat 4 side B four or five songs" use Reflextracker

## Polish Competition Scene

From multiple sources, Poland ran **dedicated Reflextracker music competitions** at demoparties:

1. **PVCF (Lemon64, 2003):** "I've a disk with a reflextracker competition from Poland" — implies
   a dedicated competition disk was produced (ca. 1996-1997 based on release year distribution)

2. **North Party 10 (Warsaw, Aug 2006):** Confirmed Reflextracker competition category.
   Three contestants: CJ Warlock (enthusiastic audience), Ramos, V-12.
   Report by V-12 at: https://riversedge.pl/north-party-10-18-20-08-2006-in-warsaw

3. **North Party competition history:** North Party was a long-running Polish C64 party
   (at least 10 editions by 2006); Reflextracker competitions likely ran from ~1996 onwards.

## Tool Longevity

Release: ~1995. Last HVSC SIDs: 2008 (3 SIDs). Competition use confirmed 2006.
Total active lifespan: ~13 years. This is exceptional for a C64 digi tracker.

The tool's longevity is attributable to:
- PC-side authoring (comfortable workflow vs. C64-native editors)
- Polish scene adoption creating a self-sustaining community
- No competing PC-cross digi tracker for C64 in the same era

## The Manual (BESCHREIBUNG)

PVCF (2003): "there should also be a manual for the tracker, if I remember right, but don't ask where."
tomaes on Pouet.net (2012): "multi page instruction note file written in German by PVCF."

The manual was:
- **Multi-page** (significant documentation)
- **Written in German** (PVCF is German; this was the primary language)
- **On the distribution disk** (file name "BESCHREIBUNG" = German for "description/instructions")
- **Written by PVCF**, not the coders (kb/Quiss/Zorc)

A separate file labelled "LSD" in the distribution refers to the **Liquid Sound Designer** —
a synth editor (not the Reflextracker digi tracker itself). PVCF confirmed these are two distinct
tools in the same release: "did you mean the 2voiced digitracker or the LSD (synth duration editor)?"
