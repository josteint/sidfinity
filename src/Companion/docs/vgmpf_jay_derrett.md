# Jay Derrett — biography and music engine

## Biography

- British programmer/composer, born 1967 or 1968.
- Discovered by CRL Group founder Clem Chambers on a tube train in
  1983 (Derrett and schoolmates were traveling to a Micromouse
  competition at a PCW show in Earl's Court, London). Chambers
  sponsored their European trip to Madrid; they won the novice round.
- Joined CRL full-time in **July 1984**.
- Began on Sinclair ZX Spectrum games solo; moved to Commodore 64
  titles, music drivers, and composition.
- Left the games industry after ~4 years (so ~1988), citing
  instability. Moved into programming for printing and finance.
- Brother Jared (now Jayenne Montana) did graphics at CRL.
- As of 2014: glider flying, conventional musical instruments,
  church activities. Self-deprecating about his game work.

## Music engine / driver

- Initially worked with "Clever Music" at CRL on score-writing.
  This relationship evolved when Derrett "thought to try writing
  some tunes himself" in the existing driver — Chambers then
  appointed him CRL's main tune writer.
- He "**reimplemented effects from Rob Hubbard and Martin Galway**,
  incorporated drum samples from *Dulcedo Cogitationis (C64)*, and
  added brush slap effects."
- Cross-platform: Amstrad CPC and ZX Spectrum 128K used the same
  format as C64. ZX Spectrum 48K format is unknown.

## Gameography (1985-1988)

The Rocky Horror Show (C64, ZXS 48K, 1985), Blade Runner (C64,
1985), Space Doubt (C64, 1986), Dracula (C64, 1986), Ballbreaker
(CPC, ZXS, 1987), Death or Glory (C64, 1987), The Equalizer (C64,
1987), Ninja Hamster (C64, CPC, 1987), Lifeforce (C64, 1987),
Mandroid (C64, Jan 1988), Thundercross (C64, Jan 1988). 30+ total.

Also co-credited on **Gun Runner (1987, The Power House)** with
Gavin Raeburn — see vgmpf_gavin_raeburn.md.

## Relevance to the Companion engine

Derrett's driver is sidid-recognised as `Companion/Jay_Derrett` —
a structural variant of the same engine family that Hubbard used
on Up Up and Away. He "reimplemented effects from Hubbard" which
fits the model that he forked/extended the Companion code from
Hubbard's 1984 work, layering on percussion samples and brush
effects as the CRL driver evolved 1985-1988.
